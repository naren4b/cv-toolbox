## 3. KEY TALKING POINTS

### Opening positioning

"I have spent the last 12 years building and running platforms at Nokia, with responsibility across Kubernetes, AWS, CI/CD, observability, reliability, and team leadership. I have led a 15-engineer DevOps and platform team and supported 8–9 products through cloud-native modernization. Earlier, at Misys, I worked directly on LoanIQ, so I understand both the lending-product context and the platform problems this role is meant to solve."

### Target Operating Platform roadmap

"I would start with a product and portfolio view: understand the deployment paths, reliability measures, platform constraints, and developer pain points for each lending product. Then I would define a small number of reference patterns for cluster operations, CI/CD, security, observability, and client deployment. The roadmap should deliver usable self-service capabilities incrementally, with adoption and reliability measures rather than only platform milestones."

### Building a centralized DevOps team

"At Nokia, I directed a 15-engineer DevOps and platform team and set standards across IaC, CI/CD, observability, and SRE. The centralized team should own the paved road, documentation, support model, and feedback loop. Product teams should consume reusable capabilities without creating a ticket for every environment or release."

### SRE and reliability

"I established SLO/SLI and golden-signal practices for mission-critical services, ran a global 24/7 on-call rotation, and led incident response and root-cause analysis. The result was 99.95% platform availability, a 35% MTTR improvement, and 40% fewer operational incidents. At Finastra, I would connect SLOs to error-budget decisions, release readiness, incident learning, and roadmap priorities."

### Client deployment standards

"I have operated platforms that included more than 1,000 customer edge Kubernetes clusters. That work required repeatable automation, environment standards, telemetry, upgrade discipline, and clear operational ownership. I would apply the same principles to client deployment patterns: validated reference architectures, versioned deployment blueprints, documented prerequisites, automated checks, and a predictable rollback path."

### Finastra and LoanIQ context

"I worked on the LoanIQ product at Misys, replacing a legacy Smalltalk implementation with a Java framework that improved performance by about 50% for banking clients. That gives me useful product context, while my later platform work gives me the operating and modernization perspective needed for a portfolio-level DevOps role."

### OpenShift, AKS, and cloud-agnostic design

"My deepest production experience is AWS EKS and on-premise Kubernetes. I would be direct about that. The operating model I use is cloud-agnostic: Terraform, Helm, Kustomize, GitOps, policy, observability, security, and automated lifecycle management. I would learn the platform-specific controls for OpenShift or AKS as part of the target-platform assessment rather than pretend the products are identical."

### Why move now

"I've spent 12 years building and running cloud platforms at Nokia. I now want a role where I own the full platform roadmap, build the operating model, and grow a team in a product company. Finastra is especially relevant because I have prior LoanIQ experience and can connect that product context with modern platform engineering."

## 4. QUESTIONS TO ASK THE INTERVIEWER

1. What is the current Target Operating Platform, and which parts are still being defined?
2. How are Lending product teams deploying today, and where is the most visible outer-loop friction?
3. Is the centralized DevOps team being built from zero, or is there an existing group to reshape?
4. Which Kubernetes platforms are in production today: EKS, OpenShift, AKS, on-premise Kubernetes, or a mix?
5. What client deployment patterns are already standardized, and where do onboarding or upgrades vary most?
6. Which SLOs and uptime commitments matter most to Corporate Lending customers?
7. How are error budgets, incident reviews, and reliability investment handled today?
8. What would success look like after six and twelve months in this role?
9. How will this platform model extend into Retail Lending?
10. What authority will this role have over hiring, tooling standards, and product-team adoption?

## 6. QUICK-WIN ACTIONS BEFORE INTERVIEW

- Prepare a 30/60/90-day plan for assessing Lending products, forming the platform roadmap, and choosing the first self-service capabilities.
- Review OpenShift concepts, especially Routes, Operators, Security Context Constraints, and differences from EKS.
- Review Backstage basics and be ready to discuss when an internal developer portal is useful versus when a focused self-service pipeline is enough.
- Prepare one concrete story about standardizing a deployment or upgrade across many customer environments.
- Prepare a simple SLO and error-budget example for a lending service, including what happens when the budget is exhausted.
- Revisit LoanIQ product context and the Misys-to-Finastra connection.
- Keep the FinOps result ready: about 15% YoY cloud-spend reduction and an estimated $500K overspend avoided.
- Keep notice-period details for recruiter discussions: 60 days, with early release open for discussion for the right role.
